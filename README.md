# Inclusive Hands Clinic

**Professional massage. Delivered by blind therapists.**

Inclusive Hands Clinic is a therapeutic massage clinic where blind and low-vision therapists work as salaried, nationally certified professionals. The first clinic is in development in Cebu City, Philippines.

## Why this project exists

In many Asian countries, massage practised by blind therapists is a recognised category of professional quality — from the tea houses of Hanoi to established clinics across Southeast Asia. Inclusive Hands Clinic builds on that precedent and runs it with the standards of a modern service business: national certification, salaried contracts and documented quality protocols.

The clinic is founded by Michele Frosi, a totally blind Italian entrepreneur. Its concept comes from lived experience: what a professional workplace must look like — tactile wayfinding, screen-reader-based tools, spoken procedures — so that blindness stops being a barrier and becomes the professional identity of the service.

## The model

- **Certified therapists.** Every therapist completes the national TESDA Massage Therapy NC II certification (about 700 hours) plus the clinic's internal assessment protocol before treating a client.
- **Real jobs.** Salaried positions above the regional minimum wage, stable contracts with full social contributions, and a career path: therapist, senior therapist, shift coordinator, academy trainer.
- **An accessible academy.** An internal training pipeline produces new therapists, with accessible materials: braille, audio and screen-reader-friendly digital formats.
- **A replicable playbook.** Every process is documented so a new clinic executes rather than reinvents. The playbook is designed to replicate the model in other countries, adapting it to each nation's certification system and regulations.

## Accessibility

This project is designed by a blind founder, and the website is built for everyone:

- Semantic HTML landmarks, logical heading order and visible focus indicators.
- A dedicated accessibility toolbar: larger or smaller text, high contrast, dark mode, readable font, underlined links and reduced motion — with preferences saved in the browser.
- Screen-reader announcements for every accessibility action (aria-live).
- Keyboard-only navigation and no auto-playing media.

If anything on the site is not accessible to you, write to us and we will fix it.
