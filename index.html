Professional massage. Delivered by blind therapists.

Inclusive Hands Clinic is a therapeutic massage clinic where blind and low‑vision therapists work as salaried, nationally certified professionals — a quality clinic first, and a workplace that proves inclusive employment works.

Partner or invest How the model works

Who we are

A founder who lives the model

Inclusive Hands Clinic is founded by Michele Frosi, a totally blind Italian entrepreneur. The clinic concept is informed by his lived experience: what a professional workplace must look like — tactile wayfinding, screen‑reader‑based tools, spoken procedures — so that blindness stops being a barrier and becomes the professional identity of the service.

The project builds on a consolidated Asian industry precedent: massage practised by blind therapists as a recognized category of quality, from the tea houses of Hanoi to established clinics across Southeast Asia. The clinic’s contribution is to run it with the standards of a modern service business: national certification, salaried contracts, documented quality protocols.

The service

The model

Certified therapists

Every therapist completes the national TESDA Massage Therapy NC II certification (about 700 hours) plus the clinic’s internal assessment protocol before treating a client.

Real jobs

Salaried positions above the regional minimum wage, stable contracts with full social contributions, and a career path: therapist, senior therapist, shift coordinator, academy trainer.

An accessible academy

An internal training pipeline produces new therapists, with accessible materials: braille, audio and screen‑reader‑friendly digital formats.

A replicable playbook

Every process is documented so a new clinic executes rather than reinvents. The playbook is designed to replicate the model in other countries, adapting it to each nation’s certification system and regulations.

The plan

The pilot: Cebu City, Philippines

The first clinic opens in Cebu, one of the country’s leading urban markets, with a diversified client base: tourists, professionals, corporate chair‑massage contracts, and clients with visual disabilities and their families — served with dedicated hours and trained staff.

  • 6–14 blind and low‑vision therapists employed over the first five years
  • Month 6 break‑even target in the base scenario
  • 100% salaried, certified therapists from day one

The full market analysis, financial model and risk matrix are documented in the business plan, available on request.

The proof

Official engagement

In April–May 2026 the project received official written replies from two Queensland Government (Australia) departments: Queensland Health, Health Workforce Division (File Ref C‑ECTF‑26/6158), and the Department of Families, Seniors, Disability Services and Child Safety (MIN ID 26‑01219 DFSDSCS 02669‑2026).

To be precise: they are official correspondence and guidance — not endorsements, nominations or funding commitments. The originals are available on request.

Accessibility statement

The toolbar at the top of this page puts the controls in your hands: enlarge or reduce the text, switch on high contrast or dark mode, apply a more readable font, underline every link and stop animations. Your choices are saved in your browser and restored on your next visit.

The website itself is built to be fully usable with screen readers and keyboard only: semantic HTML landmarks, a logical heading order, visible focus indicators, high colour contrast, resizable text and no auto‑playing media. Accessibility is not a feature of this project — it is the standard the whole service is built on.

If anything on this page is not accessible to you, write to us and we will fix it.

Chi siamo

Un fondatore che vive il modello

Inclusive Hands Clinic è fondata da Michele Frosi, imprenditore italiano totalmente cieco. Il concetto di clinica nasce dalla sua esperienza diretta: come deve essere un ambiente professionale — percorsi tattili, strumenti con screen reader, procedure vocali — perché la cecità smetta di essere una barriera e diventi l’identità professionale del servizio.

Il progetto si innesta su un precedente industriale consolidato in Asia: il massaggio praticato da terapisti non vedenti come categoria di qualità riconosciuta, dalle case da tè di Hanoi alle cliniche del Sud‑Est Asiatico. Il contributo della clinica è condurlo con gli standard di un’impresa di servizi moderna: certificazione nazionale, contratti stabili, protocolli di qualità documentati.

Il servizio

Il modello

Terapisti certificati

Ogni terapista completa la certificazione nazionale TESDA Massage Therapy NC II (circa 700 ore) più il protocollo interno di valutazione della clinica, prima di trattare un cliente.

Lavoro vero

Assunzione a tempo indeterminato con retribuzione sopra il minimo regionale, contributi completi e un percorso di carriera: terapista, terapista senior, coordinatore di turno, formatore.

Un’accademia accessibile

Una pipeline formativa interna produce nuovi operatori, con materiali accessibili: braille, audio e formati digitali compatibili con gli screen reader.

Un playbook replicabile

Ogni processo è documentato perché una nuova clinica esegua, non reinventi. Il playbook è pensato per replicare il modello in altri paesi, adattandolo al sistema di certificazione e ai regolamenti di ciascuna nazione.

Il piano

Il pilota: Cebu City, Filippine

La prima clinica apre a Cebu, uno dei principali mercati urbani del paese, con una clientela diversificata: turisti, professionisti, contratti corporate di chair massage e clienti con disabilità visive e le loro famiglie — accolti con orari dedicati e personale formato.

  • 6–14 terapisti non vedenti e ipovedenti impiegati nei primi cinque anni
  • Mese 6 obiettivo di pareggio nello scenario base
  • 100% terapisti retribuiti e certificati dal primo giorno

L’analisi di mercato completa, il modello finanziario e la matrice dei rischi sono documentati nel business plan, disponibile su richiesta.
